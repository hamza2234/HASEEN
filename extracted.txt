Abstract 
With the increasing volume of data and the growing number of features in machine 
learning problems, feature selection has become one of the most important stages of 
data preprocessing. The presence of irrelevant and redundant features  can increase 
computational complexity, reduce processing speed, and decrease the performance of 
classification models. Therefore, the use of optimization -based methods for selecting 
effective features plays a significant role in improving the accuracy and  efficiency of 
machine learning models. In this study, a hybrid method based on the PSO and GA 
metaheuristic algorithms is proposed for optimal feature selection, and its 
performance is evaluated using the Random Forest classifier. In the proposed 
approach, by simultaneously utilizing the global search capability of the PSO 
algorithm and the ability of the Genetic Algorithm to generate diverse solutions, an 
optimal subset of effective features was selected. These selected features were then 
used as inputs f or the Random Forest classification model.  To evaluate the proposed 
method, the Spambase and Phishing datasets were employed. The results showed that 
in the Spambase dataset, 46 features were selected from the initial 57 features, and the 
model achieved an accuracy of 96.79%, precision of 96.40%, recall of 97.89%, and an 
F1-score of 97.14%. In addition, the AUC value of 0.988 indicated the high capability 
of the model in distinguishing between classes. In the Phishing dataset, 22 features 
were selected from  the initial 30 features, and the classification results included an 
accuracy of 98.20%, precision of 97.98%, recall of 98.80%, and an F1 -score of 
98.39%. Furthermore, the AUC value of 0.996 demonstrated the strong discriminative 
power of the selected feat ures in separating classes.  Overall, the results of this 
research indicate that the hybrid PSO –GA method is an effective approach for feature 
selection in classification problems and can improve model performance, reduce 
computational complexity, and incre ase processing speed while simultaneously 
decreasing the number of features. 
Keywords: Feature Selection, Genetic Algorithm, Particle Swarm Optimization, 
Machine Learning, Random Forest, Classification.

Problem Statement 
With the increase in large and complex data in various problems, the presence 
of a large number of features in the data has become one of the main challenges 
in machine learning and analysis. These features can include textual, numerical, 
structural or behavioral information, each of which may have a different 
contribution to the predictio n models. Many of these features are unimportant, 
repetitive or noisy, and their use can reduce the accuracy of the models, increase 
the error rate and increase the computational complexity. In addition, the 
presence of ineffective features leads to an inc rease in the dimensions of the 
data and, as a result, longer model training time, higher consumption of 
computational resources and a decrease in the overall efficiency of the learning 
process. Therefore, the optimal and targeted selection of effective fea tures, as a 
crucial preprocessing step, plays a key role in improving the performance of 
machine learning models and can help simplify models, reduce computational 
cost, and increase prediction accuracy [4,5]. The feature selection problem can 
be considered as a multi-objective optimization problem, such that the goal is to 
simultaneously maximize prediction accuracy and minimize the number of 
features used. In practice, the search space of all possible combinations of 
features is very large and complex, an d it is impossible to explore all options. 
This has led researchers to use intelligent methods and population -based 
algorithms for feature selection, because these algorithms have the ability to 
efficiently search in a large feature space and find optimal subsets [6]. 
One of the most widely used algorithms in this field is the particle swarm 
optimization algorithm, which has a high ability to find suitable feature sets by 
utilizing group and simultaneous particle search in the solution space. However, 
the pa rticle swarm optimization algorithm may sometimes get stuck in local 
optima and fail to achieve the best answer in the search space. On the other 
hand, the genetic algorithm increases the search diversity by using combination

Methodology 
Structure and steps of the proposed method 
As stated in the introduction, in this thesi s, in order to improve the feature 
selection process and increase the accuracy of machine learning models, a 
hybrid method based on genetic algorithm and particle swarm optimization 
algorithm is used. The main goal of using this hybrid approach is to 
simultaneously utilize the fast search capability of particle swarm optimization 
algorithm and the ability of genetic algorithm to maintain population diversity 
and perform extensive search in the response space. 
In the feature selection problem, the search spa ce includes all possible subsets 
of features in the dataset, which grows exponentially with increasing number of 
features. In such circumstances, the use of traditional search methods is 
practically not possible and finding an optimal subset of features be comes a 
complex optimization problem. For this reason, the use of metaheuristic 
algorithms has been considered as an effective solution for searching in this 
large space. Combining metaheuristic algorithms can make the search process 
more efficient and inc rease the probability of achieving more optimal solutions 
by taking advantage of the advantages of each of them. 
In the proposed method of this research, the feature selection process is carried 
out using a combination of genetic algorithms and particle sw arm optimization. 
In this framework, each solution represents a subset of features and the quality 
of each subset is evaluated using the performance of a classification algorithm 
model. In this research, the random forest algorithm is used as a classificat ion 
algorithm model to measure the quality of the selected features. In this way, the 
combined algorithm tries to identify a subset of features that can provide the 
highest accuracy of the classification algorithm.

1- Data Preprocessing 
Data preprocessing is one of the important and fundamental steps in the data 
mining and machine learning process. In many real datasets, raw data usually 
has problems such as missing values, different scales between features, or noise. 
The presence of such  problems can affect the performance of machine learning 
algorithms and reduce the accuracy and efficiency of models. Therefore, data 
preprocessing is performed with the aim of preparing data and improving their 
quality before entering the modeling stage. Performing this step correctly can 
play an important role in increasing the accuracy of learning models and 
improving the feature selection process. 
In this study, before implementing the feature selection algorithm and the 
classification process, the data sets undergo several preprocessing steps to put 
the data in a format suitable for analysis and learning. The most important 
preprocessing steps in this research include missing value management, data 
normalization, and data partitioning into training and test sets. 
One of the common problems in real datasets is the presence of missing or non -
existent values in some features. The presence of these values can disrupt the 
model learning process. In this research, the three nearest neighbors method 
with a K  value of 3 is used to replace missing values. In this method, for each 
sample with a missing value, the closest samples are identified based on the 
distance criterion in the feature space, and the missing value is estimated based

3- Classification algorithm using random forest 
After an optimal subset of features was extracted in the previous step using the 
PSO GA hybrid algorithm, the next step is dedicated to training and evaluating 
the classification algorithm model based on this selected set of features. In this 
research, the random forest algorithm has been used to perform the 
classification algorithm process; a powerful and stable algorithm that, due to its 
ensemble structure, allows learni ng complex data patterns and effectively 
dealing with the overfitting phenomenon. Random forest is, in its essence, an 
ensemble learning method that is formed by putting together a large number of 
independent decision trees. Each of these trees is created based on a random 
sampling of the training data; a process known as Bagging. In this mechanism, 
to build each tree, a set of training examples is first selected with replacement 

===== PAGE 14 =====
14 
 
and then, at each node of the tree, instead of using all the features, only a 
random subset of the features is examined to find the best split. These two 
levels of randomization, namely sampling with replacement and random 
selection of features, reduce the correlation between the trees and increase the 
structural diversity of the mo del. The result of this process is a model whose 
output is the result of a set of diverse predictions and therefore has higher 
stability and accuracy. 
The training process of the random forest classifier algorithm is carried out after 
the selection of features in such a way that the training data, including the 
examples and their correct labels, is presented to the model. Each decision tree 
in the forest  forms its internal structure using the same data but with new 
random sampling. At each node, the algorithm determines the best possible split 
based on the selected features using criteria such as reducing uncertainty or 
increasing entropy. This process co ntinues until stopping criteria such as 
reaching a certain depth are met. Given the independence of tree construction, 
the entire ensemble implicitly benefits from a form of parallel learning, which 
also leads to increased computational efficiency. 
After t he construction of all trees is completed, the trained model is used to 
predict the class of new examples. In the prediction phase, each decision tree 
independently proposes an output label based on its decision paths. Then, the 
random forest determines the final class by a majority voting mechanism among 
the predictions of all trees. This voting method ensures that the final decision is 
not affected by the possible error of a single tree, and as a result, the model 
output is more stable and reliable. For t his reason, random forest usually 
provides more accurate and robust performance than a single decision tree. 
One of the key points in using random forest in this research is that the model is 
trained solely on the training data after feature selection and no test data is used 

===== PAGE 15 =====
15 
 
to build the trees at any stage. This approach prevents data leakage and makes 
the evaluation results more reliable. Since the feature set is determined by the 
PSO GA algorithm and this algorithm also uses only the training data, the 
random forest classification algorithm is also trained based on the exact same 
extracted feature space and without the interference of future information. 
It should also be noted that, unlike some progressive algorithms, random forest 
has a batch nature; th is means that after the initial training, the structure of the 
trees remains fixed and if new data needs to be changed or added, the model 
must be retrained from the beginning. In this study, in order to properly evaluate 
the performance, the random forest  model is retrained based only on the 
selected features of the same stage at each fitness calculation during the feature 
selection process. This repeated retraining ensures that the quality measurement 
of each feature subset reflects the actual behavior of  the classification algorithm 
model in future conditions. Finally, the performance of the trained model on the 
test data is reported as the final measure of the accuracy of the classification 
algorithm. 
 
Result 
As explained in the res earch methodology chapter, the first step in 
implementing the proposed method was data preprocessing. In this step, the 
missing values in the data were identified and, in order to prevent disruption in 
the learning process, their values were assigned. For this purpose, the average of 
three nearest neighbors method was used; so that the value of each missing 
feature was estimated based on the average of the values of the same feature in 
three samples close to it. Using this method, the statistical stru cture of the data 
was preserved as much as possible and bias in the data was prevented.

Next, in order to equalize the scale of the features and prevent the unbalanced 
influence of features with larger amplitudes, Min –Max normalization was 
applied to the data. In this step, the values of all features were transferred to the 
interval [0,1] to provide more suitable conditions for training machine learning 
algorithms and to make the learning process more stable and faster. 
After the preprocessing steps, the data were divided into two parts: training and 
testing. In this way, 80% of the data were used to train the model and the 
remaining 20% were used to evaluate the final performance of the model. This 
division allowed the model to be trained on the majorit y of the data, while its 
performance was evaluated independently on data that was not observed in the 
training process. 
In the next step, in order to identify effective features and eliminate unnecessary 
features, a hybrid feature selection algorithm based on a combination of particle 
swarm optimization and genetic algorithm (PSO–GA) was implemented. At this 
stage, only the data related to the training part were provided to the algorithm so 
that the feature selection process could be carried out without usi ng information 
from the test data. 
To implement the feature selection algorithm, the algorithm parameters were 
adjusted empirically. In this study, the number of algorithm iterations was set to 
20 and the population size was set to 20 members. Choosing the se values 
allowed for a proper balance between search quality and computational time; So 
that the number of iterations is sufficient for the algorithm to reach the 
appropriate areas of the search space, while the execution time of the algorithm 
does not increase too much. Also, the inertia coefficient was chosen to be equal 
to 0.7 to create a good balance between exploration of the search space and 
exploitation of promising areas. The cognitive and social learning coefficients 
were set to be equal to 1.5,  respectively, to consider the effect of the individual 

===== PAGE 17 =====
17 
 
experience of the particles as well as the best global response in a balanced way 
in the process of updating the positions. In addition, the mutation rate was 
chosen to be equal to 0.2 to prevent prem ature convergence of the algorithm by 
creating diversity in the population and to allow for the examination of more 
diverse combinations of features. In this algorithm, each response was 
represented as a binary vector of features; so that the value 1 indic ated the 
selection of the feature and the value 0 indicated its non -selection. In each 
iteration, the particle positions were modified using the particle swarm 
algorithm update relations, and then, to increase the population diversity, the 
combination and mutation operators of the genetic algorithm were applied to 
some population members. This combination allowed the algorithm to benefit 
from the fast search ability of PSO to find suitable areas and from the 
population diversity of GA to avoid getting stuck in local optima. 
The quality assessment of each feature subset was performed using the 
performance of the random forest classifier algorithm, the details of the 
objective function are provided in the research method chapter. At this stage, to 
accurately m easure the performance of the feature subsets, a five -part cross -
validation was performed, and in each part, the random forest classifier 
algorithm was trained with 100 decision trees. Finally, the average accuracy 
obtained from these steps was used as an evaluation criterion in the feature 
selection process. 
In general, in the combined PSO–GA process, the particle swarm algorithm first 
quickly identified suitable areas of the search space, and then the genetic 
operators increased the population diversity b y creating new combinations and 
applying mutations. This collaboration between the two algorithms made the 
search process more efficient and identified subsets of features that, while 
reducing the dimensionality of the data, also maintained the model's abi lity to 
perform classification.

After completing the optimization process and extracting the optimal subset of 
features by the PSO –GA hybrid algorithm, the final step of the research was 
dedicated to training and evaluating the classification algorithm model. At this 
stage, the Random Fo rest algorithm was used as a powerful ensemble learning 
model. In the configuration of the classification algorithm model, the following 
parameters were adjusted to achieve the highest efficiency: 

===== PAGE 19 =====
19 
 
1. Number of decision trees: The final model was built usin g 100 independent 
decision trees. Using this number of trees creates an optimal balance between 
the accuracy of the classification algorithm and the computational complexity 
and ensures that the variance of the model is minimized. 
2. Aggregation method: Th e training process is based on the “Bagging” 
technique. In this method, each tree is trained on a random sample with 
replacement of the training data. This diversity in the input data of each tree 
makes the model as a whole insensitive to minor changes in the data set and has 
high generalizability. 
3. Majority voting mechanism: The final class prediction is made through a 
voting process among all 100 trees. Each tree independently predicts a label and 
the class that receives the most votes is considered as the final output of the 
model. This approach neutralizes individual tree errors and significantly 
increases the final accuracy. 
4. Node splitting criterion: In the structure of each tree, the Gini index was used 
to determine the best feature for splitting nodes to minimize data impurity at 
each stage.

Conclusion 
In recent years, the increase in data volume and number of features in machine 
learning problems has made feature selection one of th e most important steps in 
data preprocessing. The presence of unnecessary and repetitive features can 
increase computational complexity, reduce processing speed, and degrade the 
performance of classification models. Therefore, using optimal methods to 
select effective features plays an important role in improving the accuracy and 
efficiency of machine learning models. In this study, a hybrid method based on 
PSO and GA metaheuristic algorithms was presented to select optimal features, 
and its performance was evaluated using the random forest classifier algorithm. 
In the proposed method, first, the PSO GA hybrid algorithm was used to search 
the feature space and find an optimal subset of effective features. The 
simultaneous use of the global search capability of the PSO algorithm and the 
ability to generate diverse responses in the genetic algorithm made the feature 


===== PAGE 30 =====
30 
 
selection process more accurate and stable. Then, the selected features were 
given to the random forest classifier algorithm to examine the perform ance of 
the model in terms of classification accuracy. 
Two datasets, Spambase and Phishing, were used to evaluate the proposed 
method. The results showed that the combined algorithm was able to remove a 
significant number of unnecessary features while main taining or improving the 
classification accuracy. In the Spambase dataset, 46 features were selected from 
among the 57 initial features, and the model was able to achieve an accuracy of 
96.79%, a precision of 96.40%, a recall of 97.89%, and an F1 score of 97.14%. 
Also, the AUC value of 0.988 indicated the high power of the model in 
separating classes. 
In the Phishing dataset, the proposed algorithm selected 22 effective features 
from among the 30 initial features. The classification results showed that the 
model achieved an accuracy of 98.20%, a precision of 97.98%, a recall of 
98.80%, and an F1 score of 98.39%. The AUC value of 0.996 also showed that 
the selected features have a very high ability to separate classes. 
In general, the results of this study sh owed that the combined PSO GA method 
is an effective approach for feature selection in classification problems and can 
improve the performance of the model while reducing the number of features. 
The use of selected features reduced the complexity of the mo del, increased the 
processing speed, and improved the generalizability of the classification 
algorithm. Also, the stability of the results in two different datasets indicates the 
reliability and appropriate efficiency of the proposed method in feature selection 
problems. 
Finally, it can be stated that feature selection using meta -heuristic algorithms, 
especially hybrid methods, can play an important role in increasing the 
efficiency of machine learning models, and the method presented in this 
research has also been able to provide acceptable results in this field.
